Geography of Sri Lanka

The Pearl-Shaped Island

Location: Sri Lanka is a pearl-shaped island nation situated in the Indian Ocean, approximately 32 km (20 miles) southwest of the Indian subcontinent. It lies between latitudes 6° and 10° N, and longitudes 80° and 87° E.

Size & Area: The island covers approximately 65,610 square kilometers (25,330 sq mi), making it the largest island in South Asia. Its coastline stretches for about 1,340 km (833 miles).

Terrain: The island features diverse landscapes including coastal plains, highlands, and central mountains. The highest peak is Pidurutalagala (Mount Pedro) at 2,524 meters (8,281 feet), located in the Central Province.

Climate: Sri Lanka has a tropical climate influenced by monsoons. The warm weather and abundant rainfall support diverse ecosystems including rainforests, tea plantations, and palm gardens.

Biodiversity: Home to unique wildlife including leopards, elephants, sloth bears, and endemic bird species. The island's biodiversity is protected through multiple national parks and reserves.

Ancient & Modern History

A Civilization Spanning Millennia

Ancient Era (125,000+ years ago): Archaeological evidence suggests human settlement dating back 125,000 years, with possible occupation extending to 500,000 years ago. The indigenous Veddahs are among the world's oldest populations.

Sinhalese Kingdom (543 BCE): According to the Mahāvamsa chronicle, Prince Vijaya arrived in 543 BCE with 700 followers, establishing the foundations of Sinhalese civilization. Buddhism was introduced in the 3rd century and became the dominant religion.

Medieval & Colonial Periods: The island flourished as a major Buddhist center and sea trade hub. Portuguese arrived in 1505, followed by the Dutch (1517-1658), and finally the British (1795-1948) who unified the entire island.

Independence: Sri Lanka gained independence on February 4, 1948, becoming a sovereign nation. It adopted its current constitution in 1978.

Modern Era: The country has developed into a major tourist destination, offering visitors a blend of ancient Buddhist heritage, colonial architecture, and natural beauty.

Rich Cultural Heritage

Buddhism & Beyond

Religion & Spirituality: Sri Lanka is predominantly Buddhist (70%), with Buddhism deeply embedded in the culture. Sacred sites like the Temple of the Tooth (Dalada Maligawa) attract pilgrims worldwide. Hinduism (13%), Islam (10%), and Christianity (7%) are also practiced.

Arts & Crafts: Traditional mask-making, wood carving, batik, and lacquerware are renowned. Ancient hydraulic engineering and architectural innovations showcase the island's historical ingenuity.

Festivals: Vesak (Buddhist New Year), Esala Perahera (grand temple festival with decorated elephants), and Tamil harvest festivals reflect the island's cultural diversity.

Cuisine: Sri Lankan food blends spices, coconut, and rice. Curries, hoppers, kottu roti, and lamprais represent the diverse culinary traditions influenced by Arab, Portuguese, Dutch, and British traders.

Languages: Sinhala (74%) and Tamil (18%) are the primary languages, with English widely spoken. The linguistic diversity reflects the island's multicultural heritage.

Warm & Welcoming People

Multi-Ethnic & Multi-Cultural Society

Ethnic Composition: Sri Lanka is home to Sinhalese (75%), Tamil (18%), and Muslim (8%) communities, creating a rich multicultural tapestry. Indigenous Veddahs represent the earliest inhabitants.

Historical Diversity: Centuries of trade and colonization brought Arabs, Portuguese, Dutch, British, and other traders, each leaving cultural imprints reflected in architecture, cuisine, and traditions.

Hospitality: Sri Lankans are renowned for exceptional warmth and hospitality. Visitors are often invited to homes for meals, and strangers are treated as guests, embodying the concept of "Suba Geethak" (auspicious welcome).

Social Values: Respect for elders, family bonds, and community cooperation are central values. Buddhism teaches compassion and non-violence, which deeply influence social customs and interactions.

Education & Development: Sri Lanka boasts a 93% literacy rate—the highest in South Asia—and significant advances in healthcare, evident in the population's long life expectancy.

Nine Provinces of Sri Lanka

Each province offers unique attractions, culture, and natural beauty

Western Province

Capital: Colombo | Population: 5.8+ million (Most densely populated)

The economic and administrative heart of Sri Lanka. Home to the Parliament in Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte. Features colonial architecture, beaches, shopping malls, and the iconic Galle Face Hotel.

Attractions: National Museum, Viharamahadevi Park, Mount Lavinia Beach

Central Province

Capital: Kandy | Population: 2.5+ million | Area: 5,674 km²

Located in the island's mountainous interior, known for tea plantations and scenic beauty. Home to the famous Sacred Temple of the Tooth, a UNESCO World Heritage site.

Attractions: Kandy Lake, Tea Plantations, Scenic Mountain Trains, Peradeniya Botanical Gardens

Southern Province

Capital: Galle | Population: 2.4+ million | Area: 5,544 km²

Renowned for pristine beaches and historic towns. Features the iconic Galle Fort, a UNESCO World Heritage site. Popular destinations include Mirissa, Unawatuna, and Matara.

Attractions: Galle Fort, Whale Watching, Turtle Conservation, Beach Resorts

Eastern Province

Capital: Trincomalee | Population: 1.5+ million | Area: 9,996 km²

Features spectacular natural harbors and mile-long beaches. Known for water sports, diving, and cultural diversity. Arugambe Beach is famous among surfers.

Attractions: Arugambe Beach (Surfing), Batticoloa Lagoon, Diving, Trincomalee Harbour

Northern Province

Capital: Jaffna | Population: 1.0+ million | Area: 8,884 km²

Known for rich Tamil cultural heritage and historical temples. The peninsula has a unique landscape and ancient sites. Home to the famous Nallur Kandaswamy Temple.

Attractions: Nallur Temple, Jaffna Fort, Naga Vihara, Island Hopping

North Central Province

Capital: Anuradhapura | Population: 1.2+ million | Area: 10,472 km² (Largest)

Home to ancient Buddhist civilization with several UNESCO World Heritage sites. The Sacred Bo tree, planted 2,300 years ago, is one of the world's oldest trees.

Attractions: Anuradhapura Ancient City, Sacred Bo Tree, Polonnaruwa Ancient City

North Western Province

Capital: Kurunegala | Population: 2.3+ million | Area: 7,888 km²

Emerging tourist destination with scenic beauty and cultural attractions. Known for paddy fields, coconut plantations, and traditional villages. Gateway to the north.

Attractions: Kurunegala Hill Fort, Dambadeniya Ancient Kingdom, Beaches, Colonial Towns

Sabaragamuwa Province

Capital: Ratnapura | Population: 1.9+ million | Area: 4,968 km²

Known as the "Gem Country" due to sapphire and gemstone mining. Features waterfalls, tea estates, and botanical gardens. Mountain scenery and adventure activities.

Attractions: Gem Mining, Waterfalls (Dunhinda, St. Clair), Tea Estates, Adventure

Uva Province

Capital: Badulla | Population: 1.2+ million | Area: 8,500 km²

Located in the eastern highlands with spectacular scenery and natural attractions. Home to several important national parks and natural reserves. Famous for tea and spices.

Attractions: Dunhinda Falls, Diyaluma Falls, Yala NP, Gal Oya NP, Badulla Temple
